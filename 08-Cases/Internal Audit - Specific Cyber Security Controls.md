# Internal Audit – Specific Cyber Security Controls

> **Historical context:** Findings and recommendations describe conditions for the audited period **December 1, 2021 to August 31, 2022** (examination May to August 2022; report dated March 2023). Do not assume the same conditions remain unresolved today unless a current official source says so. Protected finding detail must not be reconstructed. Separate **what the published report states** from any cross-domain interpretation below.


## What the published report states

**Report context.** This Audit, Evaluation, and Risk Branch report is dated March 2023. For April 1, 2021 to March 31, 2022, the report stated that the CRA administered more than $586.3 billion in revenues and pension contributions; 91% of individual and 94% of corporate returns were filed digitally, and 89% of tax services were online. Budget 2021 was stated to propose $332 million over five years and $51.2 million ongoing for cyber security.

**Objective, scope, and period.** The objective was assurance that controls were in place and working to safeguard CRA IT from digital attack. The audited period was December 1, 2021 to August 31, 2022; examination occurred from May to August 2022. The report covered a subset of higher-risk controls, not the entire cyber-security posture.

**Findings and recommendations.** The published report identified areas needing improvement, but the content is protected. It recommended that the Security Branch establish a second line of defence for cyber security to evaluate and monitor controls, and update corporate policy instruments to align with the Treasury Board Policy on Service and Digital. Other recommendations are protected.

**Management response and organizational context.** The Security Branch agreed. The report records the shared Security Branch/AERB opinion that the audit did not reveal vulnerabilities leaving the CRA open to immediate attack, while describing the audit as valuable in identifying symptoms of a larger cyber-governance gap. It states that the Security Branch was formed and that the Agency Security Officer’s role increased as of April 2022. It identifies first-line responsibility in CISD, a planned GRC capability as second line by March 2024, and AERB as third line.

**Conclusion.** The public report supplies assurance language and selected governance improvements, but does not disclose the protected finding details or the full recommendation set.

**Limitations.** This is a historical, deliberately limited audit of selected controls. It neither assesses the complete cyber posture nor supports claims about the CRA’s current vulnerabilities or control effectiveness.

> Public source limitation: Certain information was not disclosed in the published report. This vault does not attempt to reconstruct it.

## Cross-domain interpretation

This vault interprets the published organizational description through [[Defence in Depth]] and the [[Three Lines Model]]. The distinction matters: operational cyber teams can own and operate controls; an independent second-line function can set expectations, challenge assessments, and monitor control effectiveness; internal audit can provide third-line assurance. This is an interpretive learning lens, not a reconstruction of protected findings.

This vault further interprets alignment with the Treasury Board policy as a governance question connecting [[Cybersecurity]], policy instruments, risk ownership, and [[Evidence Reliability]]. A learner can ask what evidence each line needs to demonstrate that a control is designed, operating, monitored, and escalated—without inferring particular technical controls.

## Questions this case helps a learner explore

- Why might a cyber program need second-line monitoring distinct from the team operating controls?
- How can a subset-of-controls audit provide assurance without representing a complete posture assessment?
- What distinguishes a control owner’s evidence from second-line monitoring and third-line assurance?
- How does [[Defence in Depth]] combine governance, people, process, and technology controls?
- What claims are unsafe to make when a published report contains protected content?
