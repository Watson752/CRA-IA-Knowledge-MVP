# Evaluation – Audit Yield

## Official report title

Evaluation – Audit yield

## Publishing branch

[[Audit, Evaluation, and Risk Branch|AERB]]

## Report date

- Final report: **January 2020**
- Canada.ca page details: **2020-02-14** (as verified 2026-07-23)

## Evaluation framing

- Included in the Board-approved **Risk-Based Audit and Evaluation Plan 2019–2022** under *Evaluation Study – CRA’s Management of Closely-Held Business*; evaluation framework approved following the Management Audit and Evaluation Committee meeting in **May 2019**.
- Report scope expanded beyond closely-held businesses to the **full population** of income tax and GST/HST audits for the audit-yield analysis.

## Public background

The CRA annually publishes prior-year audit results, including **fiscal impact** (tax assessed, tax refunds reduced, interest and penalties, and present value of future federal tax assessable from compliance actions). Fiscal impact is reported when an audit **closes** and does **not** account for appeals reversals or uncollected amounts.

The evaluation examines whether CRA can measure **cash recovered**—amounts paid voluntarily or collected through collections—from audit **debit reassessments**, and whether a measure such as **audit yield** (OECD usage: additional tax liabilities **identified and collected** through audit) would improve performance monitoring. The report links this theme to CRA commitments following **Report 7 – Compliance Activities – Canada Revenue Agency** (2018 Fall Reports of the Auditor General).

Audit objectives cited in the report include revenue generation, trust in administration, determining true liability, disincentives to non-compliance, intelligence gathering, and identifying areas needing legislative clarification—not revenue alone.

## Evaluation objective (substantive)

Provide the Commissioner, CRA management, and the [[Board of Management]] with an independent assessment of CRA’s ability to measure the extent to which audit reassessments materialize into **cash recovered**.

## Methodology

**Definition used:** Audit yield as the **rate of recovery on accounts receivable**—actual cash paid or collected directly attributable to audit activities; a **subset** of fiscal impact excluding non-cash items such as **future taxes**.

**Income tax**

- Query approach: **85%** of T2 audit files closed **FY 2015–16** (94% of collectible taxes and penalties).
- Sample approach: **713** debit-reassessment files closed **FY 2016–17**, including all files ≥ **$5 million** (**102** files; **64%** of collectible federal taxes) plus stratified random sample of **611** others; sample segment statistically valid to population at **95% confidence** for the sampled segment.
- Broader appendix reference: **23,146** T1/T2/T3/non-resident files closed **FY 2016–17** with positive assessed tax (methodology appendix).

**GST/HST**

- Population analysis: **20,979** audit files closed **FY 2015–16** with positive GST/HST assessed; refund-only adjustments excluded; matching via Business Number and standardized accounting data (report states matching tested as accurate and reliable).

**Other methods:** Internal interviews; file and document reviews of processes and performance reports.

## What the published report states

### Finding 4.1 — Recoveries are measurable

As of **July 2019**:

| Program | Cohort | Collectible cash / recovery (report) |
|---------|--------|--------------------------------------|
| Income tax | Debit reassessments closed **FY 2016–17** | **60–64%** of collectible cash recovered; collectible portion **$6.4–6.8B** of **$9.6B** fiscal impact (**67–71%** collectible) |
| GST/HST | Debit reassessments closed **FY 2015–16** | **71%** recovered; **$1.77B** collectible of **$2.60B** fiscal impact (**68%** collectible) |

Table breakdowns include recovered, reversed, written off, under dispute, and in collections categories (income tax shown with ranges). Results expected to change as appeals conclude and collections finish; **not** performance conclusions.

Illustrative cost-effectiveness (Table 3, federal amounts): income tax **4.9:1** (**$3.09B** federal yield / **$633M** direct audit cost); GST/HST **5.9:1** (**$1.25B** / **$211M**).

### Finding 4.2 — Audit yield is relevant for monitoring

Integrated measure supports holistic view across audit, appeals, and collections; examples include cost-effectiveness ratios and downstream workload insight (Q4 reassessment value concentration).

### Finding 4.3 — Automation barriers

GST/HST: **99%** audit case-management files matched reassessments—automation feasible with Business Number.

Income tax: manual matching required; **8%** of T1 INTEGRAS debit files (**FY 2017–18**) lacked matching audit file number in assessing system; **15%** of sampled T2 AIMS files (**FY 2016–17**) unmatched (**22%** of federal tax value). Manual reassessment processing and modernizations cited as contributing factors.

### Recommendations and management response

1. **SIIB** (with CPB and others): adopt and report on an integrated measure periodically calculating cash collected from audits including downstream results (target plan completion **June 30, 2021**, contingent on recommendation 2).
2. **CPB** (with ABS, SIIB, others): changes for efficient automated cross-branch data linking (target **December 31, 2020**); multi-year plan with ITB for data gaps.

CPB and SIIB agreed; work referenced includes GST/HST methodology from prior horizontal evaluation, T1 prototype, and planned T2 prototype with longer timeframe and full data. **T3** redesign noted **out of scope** for audit yield at time of report.

### Conclusion

Audit yield complements fiscal impact with a more fulsome cash outcome story; manual cross-system matching limits repeatable query-based monitoring; expanding to multiple years needed for benchmarks.

## Public limitations

- Single-year cohort snapshots as of **July 2019**; high-dollar appeals may take years.
- Illustrative cost ratios—not prescribed CRA performance targets in the report.
- Income tax matching gaps affect population-level automation confidence.
- Does not replace a dedicated IA on tax and benefits **operations results information** (see disclaimer above).

## Cross-domain interpretation

> **Derived analysis** — not a CRA conclusion.

This vault interprets audit yield as an **integrated outcome metric** requiring explicit definitions, timing, exclusions (non-cash fiscal impact), and cross-system lineage. A performance dashboard cannot be treated as reliable without documented match rates, exception handling, and stated confidence bounds (the **95%** statement applies to the report’s specified income-tax sample segment).

Linking audit files to reassessments to accounting transactions is a **data governance** and **evidence reliability** problem, not only a reporting formula problem—consistent with themes in the BI internal audit case. Unmatched identifiers and snapshot/population limits are interpreted here as [[Missing Data]] and [[Population Completeness]] constraints that narrow how strongly agency-wide recovery conclusions can be stated; see [[How Missing Data Limits Audit Assurance]]. The evaluation period and July 2019 snapshot are historical.
