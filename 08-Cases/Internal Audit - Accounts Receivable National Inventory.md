# Internal Audit – Accounts Receivable National Inventory

> **Historical context:** Findings describe conditions for the audit period **April 1, 2022 to December 31, 2024** (examination January to March 2025; report dated May 14, 2026). Do not assume the same conditions remain unresolved today unless a current official source says so. Separate **what the published report states** from any cross-domain interpretation below.


## What the published report states

**Context.** The Accounts Receivable National Inventory (ARNI) is a Collections and Verification Branch program for tax-debt accounts below $1,000,000 that are automatically risk scored and allocated to officers. It is part of the collections continuum after automated strategies and the debt-management call centre, and before complex collections. In 2024–25, the report states ARNI resolved 171,796 accounts and collected $8.29146 billion in cash.

**Objective and scope.** The objective was assurance that controls were in place and working for effective risk scoring/file allocation and timely, progressive, appropriate collection actions. Scope covered CVB and regional controls over workload, with attention to resource use, inventory management, and account resolution. The period was April 1, 2022 to December 31, 2024; examination was January to March 2025.

**Criteria and methodology.** The audit expected documented strategic goals, aligned procedures and performance expectations; a Decision Support System (DSS) working to documented program needs; and monitoring/performance controls aligned to objectives. Methods were documentation review in CVB and four regions, data analytics using workload-management, DSS, Revenue Enforcement Management and Information Tracking System, and data-mart data, plus interviews with selected staff.

**Findings.** The audit concluded that controls existed for risk scoring/allocation and appropriate collection actions, but processes to monitor that controls worked and address performance issues were limited. It found account churn was not formally defined during examination, and the HQ report did not always reflect movement or program production completely. ARNI Taxpayer Services indicators did not directly contribute to churn, cash collected, or resolution. Roles/processes to assess intended versus actual outcomes of business rules were undocumented, and regional processes affecting allocation/churn had limited controls.

Quality assurance/review tools and guidance were in place. However, the DSS had limited enterprise-level controls for allocating accounts to the right agents and for using system-generated BI; regions used local tools and had been directed not to use DSS data for reporting/decisions. The report also found incomplete performance measures: in 2023–24, 20% of accounts reported as resolved while unassigned/self-resolved had previously been assigned to ARNI, representing about $604.9 million in cash collected; 611 collapsed inventories contained 128,958 accounts. It reported 56% of payment arrangements were suspended at least once, without an apparent HQ/regional mechanism to monitor or address that frequency.

**Recommendations and MAPs.** CVB agreed to define and monitor churn, outputs, officer expectations, and responsive reporting; align ARNI Taxpayer Services objectives/indicators; document governance for business rules; promote DSS use and identify an enterprise solution; and document roles and processes for regional inventory effects. Action-plan targets range from September 2026 through March 2030. The report records a formal churn definition and planning guidelines issued during the audit in May 2025.

**Conclusion.** The historical conclusion separates controls that existed from reported gaps in management controls, decision-support use, and complete/actionable reporting.

**Limitations.** This report does not establish present ARNI performance or the results of future action plans. “Resolved” has a report-specific definition that can include full payment, write-off, or transfer to a higher-complexity workload; it must not be read as cash collected alone.

## Cross-domain interpretation

This vault interprets ARNI as a [[Performance Reporting]] case in which a useful headline metric needs a clear numerator, denominator, business rules, attribution rules, and known exclusions. The report’s examples show why a result can be operationally meaningful yet incomplete for a particular decision—treated here as a [[Missing Data]] and [[Population Completeness]] problem when movement, attribution, or collapsed-inventory views are omitted from the measure used for the decision. See [[How Missing Data Limits Audit Assurance]]. Historical findings are not a claim about current ARNI completeness.

This vault also interprets the DSS finding through [[Business Intelligence]], [[Data Governance]], and [[Evidence Reliability]]. Risk scoring, allocation, local workarounds, and reported outcomes form a feedback loop: a decision-support system needs documented ownership, validation, monitoring of intended versus actual results, and usable reporting before its data can support enterprise learning.

## Questions this case helps a learner explore

- How would you define account churn so it remains comparable across regions and time?
- What reconciliation would show where production credit changes when an account moves?
- Which indicators distinguish cash collection, resolution, inventory movement, and effort on complex referrals?
- How could controls test whether risk scoring and allocation support the intended operational outcomes?
- What information must be complete before a dashboard is decision-useful?
