# Internal Audit – Charities Audit Process

> **Historical context:** Findings describe conditions for the audit period **April 1, 2020 to March 31, 2023** (examination June 2023 to January 2024; report dated January 2025 in vault frontmatter). Do not assume the same conditions remain unresolved today unless a current official source says so. Separate **what the published report states** from any cross-domain interpretation below.


## What the published report states

**Context.** The Charities Directorate (CD) within the Legislative Policy and Regulatory Affairs Branch (LPRAB) promotes compliance with legislation relating to charities through education and enforcement. Its Compliance Division manages general charities compliance, while the Review and Analysis Division (RAD) manages a program focused on terrorism-abuse and non-compliance risks. The report states that approximately 86,000 Canadian charities were federally registered and that the two divisions completed 545 audits from 2020–21 through 2022–23: 541 by Compliance and 4 by RAD.

**Objective and scope.** The audit sought assurance that key audit processes were in place and followed for the impartial conduct of CD audits. Scope included planning and information gathering, risk assessment, workload identification, audit methodologies, and monitoring/oversight. It excluded a broader effectiveness review, detailed review of how audits were conducted, and broader AML-ATF or national-security activities outside RAD. The period was April 1, 2020 to March 31, 2023; examination was June 2023 to January 2024.

**Criteria and methodology.** Criteria covered: (1) established and communicated processes, documented/reviewed plans and risk approaches, and controls over information, leads, BI, and potential audit-file assessment/approval; and (2) monitoring for impartiality plus oversight mechanisms. Methods included document review; interviews and walkthroughs at headquarters and regional offices; review of control outcomes and supporting documentation, including taxpayer and classified information; application-data analysis for statistics and indicators; review of reporting; and meetings coordinated with Security Branch and NSIRA.

**Findings.** Workflows, plans, and much oversight were established, but improvements were needed to demonstrate impartiality and consistency. RAD risk indicators were last updated and approved in December 2016, without a formal periodic-review requirement. The report found gaps in formal information-gathering processes, review checkpoints, standards, terminology, documentation of risk levels and treatments, and documented approvals. It noted manual work across systems, limited/incomplete data dispersed across sources, and limited ability to report on impartiality across the population. File reviews covered 21 risk-assessed cases and 18 completed audits; documented reviews and approvals were not always complete. Monitoring largely used operational metrics and did not explicitly cover impartiality and consistent treatment across cases.

**Recommendations and MAPs.** LPRAB was recommended to require periodic review/approval of RAD risk indicators; formalize roles, procedures, quality review, detailed criteria/tools, and decision documentation; improve information systems/BI strategy/reporting; and expand data gathering and monitoring/reporting for impartiality. LPRAB agreed. Action plans included documented interdepartmental/external-review outcomes and formal risk-indicator review (March 2026); roles, job aids, checkpoints, RAD procedures, and risk recording (targets March 2025 to March 2026); modernization/BI options and consultations (to March 2026); and quality-assurance and monitoring improvements (to September 2025).

**Conclusion.** The report concluded that workflows were communicated and planning/oversight generally existed, but the manually intensive, judgment-dependent process required stronger controls to demonstrate impartial and consistent decisions.

**Limitations.** This report’s findings concern the reviewed period and scope. It contains redacted material, and this note does not summarize classified or taxpayer information.

## Cross-domain interpretation

This vault interprets the report as an operational [[Data Quality]] and [[Evidence Reliability]] case: impartiality is supported not only by a decision’s rationale, but by structured inputs, consistent terminology, retained approvals, traceable review evidence, and population-level monitoring. Manual transfers across systems increase the need for explicit reconciliation and quality controls; this is not a claim that automation alone assures fairness.

This vault interprets the report’s limited/incomplete data dispersed across sources, and limited population-level impartiality reporting, as a [[Missing Data]] and [[Population Completeness]] concern: a performance view based only on open/closed files and outcomes may not show whether comparable cases received comparable treatments. The reported model implemented in April 2024 and planned actions are management responses, not evidence that historical gaps were already resolved. See [[How Missing Data Limits Audit Assurance]].
