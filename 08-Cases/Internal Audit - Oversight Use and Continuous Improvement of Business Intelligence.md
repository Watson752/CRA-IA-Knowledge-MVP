# Internal Audit — Oversight, Use, and Continuous Improvement of Business Intelligence

Internal Audit – Oversight, Use, and Continuous Improvement of Business Intelligence

## Publishing branch

[[Audit, Evaluation, and Risk Branch|AERB]]

- Coverage: **1 April 2020 – 31 March 2023**

## Public background

The published report states that the CRA creates and collects taxpayer information and holds millions of taxpayer data records used for purposes including developing [[Business Intelligence]] to support compliance, collections, and verification.

The report defines business intelligence as information derived from available data that provides knowledge and insights to support decision-making and program administration. BI may include reporting, [[Analytics]], and [[Research]].

[[Service, Innovation, and Integration Branch|SIIB]] has held responsibility for BI activities since 2011, with service delivery by [[Information Technology Branch|ITB]]. The [[Chief Data Officer]] is also the Assistant Commissioner of SIIB. The report notes decentralized BI capacity across branches and regions as demand grew after 2011.

In 2020 the CRA introduced the [[Information and Data Strategy]], with four pillars: governance, people and culture, environment and infrastructure, and supporting innovation and transformation.

## Importance

The report states the audit is important because CRA BI capacity expanded rapidly and the CRA committed to optimizing data use, facilitating sharing and collaboration, increasing usability and reusability, and supporting [[Evidence-Based Decision-Making]].

## Audit objective

Provide the Commissioner, CRA management, and the [[Board of Management]] with assurance that business intelligence is being overseen and used, and continuously improved, to support compliance, collections, and verification programs.

## Scope

**In scope:** governance structures, uses, and continuous improvement of business intelligence.

**Out of scope (as stated):** examination of data security; human resource challenges; applications, tools, and infrastructure and their lifecycle used to develop BI.

## Audit criteria

Line of enquiry (Appendix A): a management control framework is in place for BI to support compliance, collections, and verification, including documented framework elements (data governance; responsible business areas; enterprise and branch/regional strategies; roles/responsibilities/accountability; approval levels; monitoring and reporting; leadership), clarity of roles for senior management/stakeholders, an oversight process for enterprise-view tool acquisition aligned with the Information and Data Strategy, and a structure fostering continuous improvement.

## Methodology

Documented methodology included:

- Document review of roles, strategies, policies, terms of reference, and IT architecture governance records
- Process/methodology review of selected CPB and CVB teams using BI
- Interviews with stakeholders in SIIB, ITB, CVB, CPB, DTPB, and BI Quality Assurance in Western, Ontario, Quebec, and Atlantic regions
- Observation of governance committee and working-group meetings

Conducted in accordance with the International Standards for the Professional Practice of Internal Auditing.

## What the published report states

### Overall conclusion

Important components of the BI framework are in place; some improvements are needed to strengthen it.

### Published findings (paraphrased)

1. **Governance framework:** Documented governance exists for management of data and acquisition of tools. Actions required: establish CRA-wide objectives for BI; clarify [[Roles and Responsibilities]]; determine a common definition of BI.
2. **Strategy:** The BI strategy evolved toward data governance and tool acquisition but was missing strategies to leverage and coordinate BI horizontally.
3. **Working groups / collaboration:** Working groups foster continuous improvement, but limited horizontal collaborations were observed; developers often worked in silos; many workload teams collaborated only within program/branch groups.
4. **Continuous improvement:** No CRA-wide expectations for developing BI; inconsistent continuous improvement processes; instances where potentially outdated BI was not reviewed and updated for multiple years.
5. **Tool deployment:** An oversight body/process exists for enterprise view when acquiring tools, but no formal process supported effective and timely deployment to BI teams Agency-wide.

### Published recommendations

SIIB, in collaboration with ITB and other stakeholders, should strengthen the BI governance framework by:

1. Establishing CRA-wide BI objectives, clear roles and responsibilities, and a common BI definition
2. Developing a strategy to coordinate, leverage, and monitor BI efforts horizontally so BI meets program decision-making needs and supports CRA strategic priorities
3. Establishing expectations for developing BI, including continuous improvement requirements
4. Developing an effective deployment process for timely delivery of BI tools

### Published management responses and action-plan dates

SIIB agrees; AERB judged action plans reasonable. Public action-plan commitments include (paraphrased):

- **By March 2025:** AC-level collaboration to establish enterprise-wide BI objectives/direction; document definitions, roles/responsibilities, links to AI Enablement Plan and Information and Data Strategy
- **By June 2025:** Enhance/coordinate/leverage/monitor BI horizontally (including cloud capability strategy work with ITB)
- **By June 2025:** Scan BI areas and peer international organizations; plan to strengthen BI community of practice
- **By December 2024:** Review last BI tool deployment and identify steps for a new formal process

### Conclusion (report)

Restates that important framework components exist and that improvements are needed in objectives/roles/definition, horizontal strategy, collaboration, continuous improvement expectations, and tool deployment process.
