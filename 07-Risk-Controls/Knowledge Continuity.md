**Knowledge continuity** is the ability to retain and transfer critical know-how when staff change roles, retire, or leave— so operations, [[Control]]s, and analytics remain effective. Loss of continuity drives [[Siloed Knowledge]], [[Outdated Analytics]], and repeated errors despite prior audit [[Finding]]s.

Practices include documented procedures, cross-training, mentorship, repositories for governed definitions, and succession planning for key [[Control Ownership]] roles. [[Horizontal Collaboration]] spreads expertise beyond single points of failure.

Technology helps but does not replace curation— [[Unstructured Information]] in wikis requires maintenance like code. [[Continuous Improvement]] captures lessons from incidents and audits into accessible playbooks.

Auditors may treat continuity as a risk when specialized systems lack backup expertise or when remediation knowledge walked out the door after [[Follow-up]] closed items.
