**Content class:** general professional concept. The public CRA Organization page does not use this exact title as a formal org-chart label; in vault practice, accountability often appears as a named program area, OPI, or MAP owner in a published audit.

A **business process owner** is accountable for a defined end-to-end process and its **business outcomes**— not for building every system that supports it. Owners set process requirements, accept residual risk within policy, ensure controls fit the process, and respond when audits find gaps. This differs from [[Technical Support]] (technology delivery) and from [[Control Ownership]] when a single control is owned separately from the whole process.

In public-sector internal audit, the “owner” may be a branch, director general area, or delegated official named in the report. [[CRA-Regions]] may deliver field work while headquarters program areas retain policy or oversight accountability; case language varies.

Worked pattern (case-specific, not a universal CRA rule): [[Internal Audit - Oversight Use and Continuous Improvement of Business Intelligence]] names [[Service, Innovation, and Integration Branch|SIIB]] as holding BI activity responsibility and MAP lead, while [[Information Technology Branch|ITB]] delivers BI services— illustrating process accountability separate from technical delivery.

For technology-enabled processes, process owners typically set business requirements and exception/override **policy**, accept residual risk, and ensure monitoring ownership is named—without owning independent assurance ([[How Organizational Ownership Affects System Accountability]]). They do not automatically administer systems ([[System Owner]], [[Technical Support]]) or execute AERB remediation.
