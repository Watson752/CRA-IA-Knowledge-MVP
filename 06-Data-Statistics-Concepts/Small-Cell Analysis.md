**Small-cell analysis** examines table cells or subgroups with very few observations. Estimates from small cells have high variance and may violate disclosure rules, triggering [[Data Suppression]]. Inferential statements from tiny samples are fragile and easily distorted by outliers.

Analysts may combine categories, use moving averages, or apply hierarchical models— each with assumptions that must be stated. [[Sampling Risk]] rises when audits sample within sparse strata.

[[Research]] ethics also limit reporting on identifiable small groups. Auditors should avoid over-interpretation when exceptions in small cells drive narrative [[Finding]]s.

Link to [[How Statistical Limitations Affect Audit Conclusions]] when conclusions rest on sparse data.
