**Risk algorithms** are rules, scoring models, or machine learning systems that rank entities, transactions, or cases by perceived [[Risk]] for prioritization or action. Tax and benefits agencies, financial institutions, and regulators commonly use such models for workload allocation—not necessarily as final legal determinations.

Algorithmic risk systems raise governance questions: data bias, transparency, validation, override processes, and periodic recalibration. Errors can affect fairness and public trust. Reviewers examine documentation, testing results, and [[Monitoring and Reporting]] of model performance drift.

Auditors evaluate controls around model changes, access to parameters, and segregation between developers and operators. Links to [[Artificial Intelligence]], [[Data Quality]], and [[Population Completeness]] are common when training data omit segments of the population.

Concept notes do not describe specific organizational models; case studies may reference publicly documented approaches when sources exist.
