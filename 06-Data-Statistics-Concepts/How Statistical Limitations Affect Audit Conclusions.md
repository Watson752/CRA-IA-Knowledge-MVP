Auditors increasingly rely on aggregate statistics, analytics, and sampled data. **Statistical limitations**— revision policy, suppression, rounding, sparse cells, changing definitions, and measurement error— constrain how strongly conclusions may be stated. Professional skepticism requires matching assertion strength to evidence strength.

[[Missing Data]] interacts with these limits: accidental gaps reduce [[Data Quality]] and [[Population Completeness]], while [[Data Suppression]] and redaction withhold values by design. Neither should be treated as identical, but both can force directional rather than precise conclusions.

When audit objectives reference public performance or statistical indicators, teams should read methodological footnotes, understand [[Assessment Cut-Off Date]] and [[Statistical Revision]], and assess [[Comparability Across Editions]]. A variance may reflect data process limits rather than control failure.

Limitations should appear in report scope or methodology sections: what was verified, what was taken from published sources as-is, and where conclusions are directional rather than precise. This reduces overreach and supports defensible [[Finding]] language.

Bridge concept linking [[Evidence Reliability]], [[Data Quality]], and [[Performance Reporting]] to core audit communication duties. Cross-domain consequences: [[How Missing Data Limits Audit Assurance]].
