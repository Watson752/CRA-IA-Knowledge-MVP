**Data quality** describes fitness for use across dimensions such as [[Data Accuracy|accuracy]], completeness, consistency, [[Data Timeliness|timeliness]], validity, and [[Record Uniqueness|uniqueness]]. Keep these dimensions distinct—especially accuracy vs completeness. Poor quality undermines [[Analytics]], [[Risk Algorithms]], [[Management Reporting]], and audit [[Evidence]]. Quality is contextual: data adequate for operational processing may be insufficient for statistical publication or forensic audit.

[[Missing Data]] is a primary way completeness fails: expected records, fields, periods, or supporting evidence are absent. That weakness can cascade into [[Population Completeness]] problems and lower [[Evidence Reliability]], so auditors document quality limitations when relying on data extracts and may test quality rules directly.

Management typically combines preventive controls (validations at entry), detective controls (profiling, [[Data Reconciliation|reconciliations]]), and remediation workflows. [[Data Governance]] assigns stewardship and defines critical data elements. Pipeline stages ([[Data Pipeline]]) are common quality failure points (filters, [[Rejected Records]], bad [[Reference Data]]).

Related concepts include [[Population Completeness]], [[Initial Assessment Data]] versus [[Reassessment Data]], and [[Statistical Revision]]. Improving data quality is a [[Continuous Improvement]] theme tied to [[Enterprise Objectives]].
