**Evidence-based decision-making** uses relevant, reliable information— data, [[Research]], professional judgment, and stakeholder input— to choose actions. It does not mean data alone decides; it means choices are transparent about evidence used and limitations acknowledged.

Enablers include [[Data Quality]], governed [[Business Intelligence]], skilled [[Analytics]], and culture that welcomes disconfirming facts. Barriers include [[Outdated Analytics]], [[Siloed Knowledge]], and pressure for quick answers.

Public sector context adds legal constraints, fairness, and transparency duties. Audit functions contribute evidence on controls and performance but respect management’s decision authority.

Auditors may evaluate whether programs monitor outcomes and adjust based on metrics versus anecdote— without prescribing specific policy choices.
