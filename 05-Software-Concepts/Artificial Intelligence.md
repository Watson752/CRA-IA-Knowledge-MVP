**Artificial intelligence** refers to systems that perform tasks requiring human-like perception, reasoning, or learning— including machine learning models, large language models, and decision automation. In organizations, AI supports classification, forecasting, natural language interfaces, and operational optimization. AI introduces distinct risks: model bias, drift, opaque reasoning, data leakage, and over-reliance on probabilistic outputs.

Governance spans data suitability ([[Data Quality]]), training and validation documentation, human oversight, security ([[Cybersecurity]]), and alignment with [[Criteria]] and law. AI outputs are generally not standalone audit [[Evidence]] without validation against source data and controls.

[[Risk Algorithms]] used in compliance or prioritization contexts may warrant heightened review for fairness, explainability, and [[Population Completeness]]. [[Tool Acquisition]] and [[Tool Deployment]] processes should cover model lifecycle and monitoring.

Auditors and evaluators increasingly examine whether AI use is documented, authorized, and monitored— themes that case studies may explore when tied to public sources.
