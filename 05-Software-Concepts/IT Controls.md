**IT controls** are [[Control]]s over information technology general controls (ITGCs) and application controls that support reliable processing, security, and change management. ITGCs commonly include [[Identity and Access Management|access management]], [[Change Management|change/release management]], backup and recovery, job scheduling, and [[Segregation of Duties]] in administrative functions. Application controls enforce [[Automated Business Rules|business rules]] at transaction level (validations, approvals, reconciliations)—see [[Automated Control]].

Distinguish [[Excessive Privileges]] from [[Unauthorized Access]] when testing access. Weak ITGCs can undermine many application controls simultaneously— a central theme in IT audit. COBIT and similar frameworks organize IT governance and management objectives; [[Security Controls]] address confidentiality, integrity, and availability specifically.

Auditors often test ITGCs before relying on automated application controls or [[Analytics]] over [[Structured Data]]. [[Tool Deployment]] and [[Tool Acquisition]] processes are evaluated for governance and security integration. Pipeline reliability also depends on change and access over jobs/datasets ([[Data Pipeline]]).

IT controls connect to [[Three Lines Model]] roles: operations runs controls, IT security and enterprise architecture provide second-line guidance, internal audit provides assurance.
