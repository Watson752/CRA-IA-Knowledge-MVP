**Criteria** are the benchmarks or standards against which audit subject matter is evaluated. They may be laws, regulations, policies, professional standards, control frameworks, service-level agreements, budgets, or internally adopted performance targets. Without agreed criteria, auditors cannot consistently determine whether conditions represent deficiencies, exceptions, or acceptable variation.

Criteria should be suitable, available, and clearly linked to the audit objective and scope. Suitable criteria are relevant, reliable, and understandable to intended users. When multiple criteria apply (e.g., legal minimum versus best practice), the audit report should clarify which were used and how conflicts were resolved.

In internal audit, criteria often include organizational policies, risk appetite statements, and control design expectations. External assurance engagements may reference COSO, COBIT, ISO standards, or sector-specific requirements. Auditors document how criteria were selected and whether management agreed to them.

Findings typically describe a condition, compare it to criteria, identify cause where practicable, and assess effect or risk. Weak or unstated criteria undermine the defensibility of [[Finding]]s and [[Recommendation]]s.
