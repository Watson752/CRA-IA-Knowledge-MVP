**Sampling risk** is the risk that conclusions drawn from a sample differ from what would be concluded if the entire population were tested. It arises in statistical and non-statistical sampling when auditors extrapolate from selected items to a broader [[Scope]]. Sampling risk interacts with [[Risk]] assessment and the desired level of assurance.

Auditors manage sampling risk through sample design (random selection, stratification), size, and evaluation of exceptions. Non-representative selection (convenience or bias toward known problems) increases risk beyond formal statistical formulas. When exceptions are found, projection methods estimate population error rates with stated confidence.

Some engagements use full-population testing when data and tools allow ([[Analytics]] on [[Structured Data]]), reducing sampling risk but introducing other limitations ([[Data Quality]], completeness). [[Missing Data]] in the frame or in key fields can make a sample non-representative even when formal selection looks random, and incomplete populations inflate sampling risk via [[Population Completeness]]. Reports should state when findings are sample-based and whether results were projected.

Related statistical concepts include [[Population Completeness]] and [[Small-Cell Analysis]] when samples or populations are sparse.
