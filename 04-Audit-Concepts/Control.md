A **control** is a policy, procedure, practice, or mechanism designed to provide reasonable assurance that objectives will be achieved and risks mitigated. Controls may be preventive (stop errors or fraud before they occur), detective (identify issues after they occur), or corrective (remediate and improve). They operate at entity, process, and transaction levels.

Auditors evaluate **design effectiveness** (whether the control, if operated as intended, would address the risk) and **operating effectiveness** (whether it actually functioned over the period examined). Control testing may use inquiry, observation, inspection, reperformance, or data analytics. Weak or missing controls increase [[Risk]] and may support [[Finding]]s when paired with evidence of adverse conditions.

Control frameworks (e.g., COSO internal control components, COBIT for IT) help structure reviews. [[IT Controls]] and [[Security Controls]] are specialized subsets. Clear [[Control Ownership]] supports accountability for design, operation, and remediation.

Controls are not guarantees; they reduce risk to an acceptable level within cost and operational constraints. Over-reliance on manual controls, compensating controls, or undocumented practices are common review themes in professional audit literature.
