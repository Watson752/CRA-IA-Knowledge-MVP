**Internal audit and program evaluation** functions provide independent assurance and insight on governance, risk management, controls, and program performance. Internal audit typically follows professional standards (e.g., IIA Global Internal Audit Standards), emphasizes objectivity and a systematic methodology, and reports to audit committees or equivalent governance bodies. Program evaluation focuses on relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, and sometimes equity of programs, often using mixed methods and logic models.

Both disciplines share themes: clear [[Audit Objective]] and [[Scope]], defensible [[Evidence]], transparent [[Finding]]s, and actionable [[Recommendation]]s. Evaluation may place greater weight on outcomes measurement, counterfactual reasoning, and stakeholder perspectives; internal audit may emphasize control design and compliance with [[Criteria]]. In practice, functions may coexist in one shop or collaborate on integrated reviews.

Federal departments and agencies in Canada commonly publish selected internal audit and evaluation reports for transparency. The Canada Revenue Agency, like other organizations, may publish such reports on its public website; this vault may link official landing pages and case studies when source notes exist—see [[SRC-CRA-IA-PE-Landing]] when created in the organization or sources folders.
