The **audit objective** states what the audit is intended to accomplish: the subject matter being examined and the purpose of the examination. It answers why the audit is being performed and what assurance or insight stakeholders should gain. A clear objective anchors planning, scope, criteria, and reporting so that work remains focused and conclusions remain traceable to the stated intent.

Objectives are typically expressed in terms of evaluating whether controls, processes, or outcomes meet defined criteria within a specified scope. They may seek reasonable assurance that risks are managed appropriately, that compliance requirements are met, or that resources are used efficiently and effectively. Precision matters: vague objectives invite scope creep and make it difficult to judge whether the audit succeeded.

In internal audit, objectives align with the organization's risk profile and the internal audit function's charter. They should be agreed or communicated to management and, where relevant, those charged with governance before fieldwork begins. When objectives change materially during the engagement, the change should be documented and understood by stakeholders.

Objectives differ from scope (what is included and excluded) and from criteria (the benchmarks used to evaluate the subject matter). Together, objective, scope, and criteria form the engagement specification that drives methodology and evidence requirements.
