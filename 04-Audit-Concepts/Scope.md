**Scope** defines the boundaries of an audit: which entities, processes, systems, time periods, locations, and activities are included—and explicitly what is excluded. Scope sets expectations for stakeholders about the depth and breadth of examination and prevents misunderstandings about what was and was not reviewed.

Scoping decisions reflect the audit objective, available resources, risk prioritization, and materiality to the organization. Limitations on scope (e.g., restricted access to data, systems not in production, or a sample rather than full population) should be documented so readers can interpret findings and conclusions appropriately. Exclusions do not automatically imply those areas are low risk; they may reflect timing, access, or plan constraints.

Scope may evolve during planning as risk assessment refines focus areas. Material changes should be communicated and recorded. In performance and compliance audits, scope often ties to legislative mandates, program boundaries, or control frameworks under review.

Effective scope statements use plain language: named programs or processes, fiscal or calendar periods, geographic or organizational units, and any reliance on third parties or shared services. Scope works with [[Criteria]] and [[Methodology]] to determine what [[Evidence]] is needed.

## Office of Primary Interest (OPI) and audit client

**Content class:** general professional / vault onboarding vocabulary. [[CRA-Acronym-Dictionary]] records **OPI** as the business area primarily responsible for the audited subject. Related teaching term: [[Audit Client]].

In this vault’s public-case practice:

- **OPI / program owner** — area named as primarily responsible for the audited subject (when the report uses that language).
- **MAP / management-action owner** — management area that agrees to and owns corrective action ([[Management Action Plan Owner]]).
- **Publishing assurance branch** — typically [[Audit, Evaluation, and Risk Branch|AERB]]; not the OPI.

OPI and MAP owner are often the same area, but confirm each report’s labeled organizational section. Do not infer OPI from topic similarity alone. See [[Ownership and Assurance Roles]].
