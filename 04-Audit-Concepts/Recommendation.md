A **recommendation** is auditor guidance on actions management should consider to address a [[Finding]], reduce [[Risk]], or strengthen [[Control]]s relative to [[Criteria]]. Good recommendations are specific, actionable, feasible, and linked to root cause rather than symptoms. They should identify who should act (often via [[Control Ownership]] or process owners) without usurping management’s responsibility to decide how to run the organization.

Recommendations vary in urgency and may include short-term compensating actions and longer-term structural fixes. Auditors may offer alternative approaches when trade-offs exist (cost, operational impact, technology constraints). Where management disagrees, responses are captured in [[Management Response]] rather than silently dropping the issue.

Internal audit standards emphasize that recommendations are advisory; implementation choices belong to management and governance. [[Follow-up]] audits verify whether agreed actions were implemented and whether risk was reduced as intended.

Recommendations should avoid vague language (“improve monitoring”) in favor of measurable outcomes (“implement quarterly access reviews for privileged accounts with documented sign-off”).
