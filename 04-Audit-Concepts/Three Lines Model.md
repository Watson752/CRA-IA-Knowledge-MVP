The **Three Lines Model** (formerly “Three Lines of Defense”) describes how organizations structure risk management and control accountability. **First line** functions own and manage risks and controls in day-to-day operations. **Second line** functions provide expertise, monitoring, and policy (e.g., risk management, compliance, IT security) to support and challenge the first line. **Third line** internal audit provides independent assurance on governance, risk management, and controls.

The model emphasizes coordination, not rigid silos. Second-line activities (e.g., continuous monitoring) differ from third-line assurance in independence and mandate. Clear roles reduce gaps and duplication—themes related to [[Roles and Responsibilities]] and [[Horizontal Collaboration]].

Internal audit reports to those charged with governance to preserve independence while working constructively with management. The model aligns with [[Governance]] and [[Risk Management]] expectations in public and private sectors.

Auditors use the model to scope reliance on second-line work, assess control ownership, and frame [[Finding]]s without blurring accountability between lines.
