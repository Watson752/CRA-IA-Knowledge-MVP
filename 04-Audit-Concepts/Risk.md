In audit and assurance, **risk** is the possibility that events or conditions will adversely affect the achievement of objectives. Auditors consider inherent risk (before controls), control risk (that controls will not prevent or detect issues), and detection risk (that audit procedures will not detect material misstatements or significant weaknesses). Risk thinking prioritizes where limited audit resources should focus.

Risk is often described in terms of likelihood and impact on objectives such as financial reporting, compliance, operations, reputation, or public trust. Enterprise risk management frameworks categorize strategic, operational, financial, and compliance risks. Internal audit uses risk assessment to build annual plans and to scope individual engagements.

Audit findings frequently express **residual risk**—the risk remaining after considering the effectiveness of existing [[Control]]s—or the risk implied by a gap between condition and [[Criteria]]. Not every control weakness constitutes a reportable finding; significance depends on context, aggregation, and tolerance set by governance.

Linking risk to root cause and effect helps management and those charged with governance decide on [[Recommendation]]s and [[Management Action Plan]] priorities. See also [[Risk Management]] and [[Sampling Risk]] for related ideas.
